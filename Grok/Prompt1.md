**AI (Artificial Intelligence)** is the field of computer science focused on creating systems that can perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence. These tasks include learning from experience, understanding natural language, recognizing patterns, solving problems, making decisions, and perceiving the world through senses like vision or sound.

### Core Idea
At its heart, AI is about building machines that **think** and **act**—either by mimicking human cognition (the "thinking humanly" approach) or by achieving intelligent outcomes through any effective method (the "rational agent" approach). Modern AI doesn't need to be conscious or sentient; it succeeds by optimizing for specific goals using data, algorithms, and massive computation.

### Main Types of AI

| Type                  | Description                                      | Examples Today                  | Status                  |
|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Narrow AI (ANI)**  | Specialized for one task                        | ChatGPT, image generators, recommendation algorithms, self-driving features | Widely deployed        |
| **General AI (AGI)** | Human-level intelligence across any intellectual task | None (yet)                     | Research goal          |
| **Superintelligence**| Surpasses human intelligence in all domains     | Hypothetical                   | Speculative/future     |

Most "AI" you interact with today is **narrow AI**, powered heavily by **machine learning** (especially deep learning).

### Key Technologies Powering Modern AI
- **Machine Learning (ML)**: Systems improve performance through data and experience rather than explicit programming.
- **Deep Learning**: Uses multi-layered neural networks inspired (loosely) by the brain. Excels at pattern recognition in images, speech, and text.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs)**: Like me (Grok), trained on enormous text datasets to understand and generate human-like language.
- **Other branches**: Computer vision, reinforcement learning (learning through trial and reward), robotics, expert systems, etc.

### Brief History
- **1950s**: Birth of the field (Dartmouth Conference 1956, Alan Turing's ideas).
- **1960s-80s**: Early optimism, followed by "AI winters" due to overhype and under-delivery.
- **2010s-now**: Explosion driven by big data, powerful GPUs, and the transformer architecture (2017), which revolutionized language and multimodal AI.

### What AI Is Good At (2026)
- Generating text, code, images, video, and music.
- Analyzing massive datasets faster than humans.
- Playing games (chess, Go, StarCraft).
- Assisting in medicine, science, engineering, and creative work.
- Automating repetitive cognitive tasks.

### Limitations
- No true understanding or consciousness—just sophisticated pattern matching and prediction.
- Can hallucinate (make things up confidently).
- Biased by training data.
- High energy/computational cost.
- Struggles with true novelty, common-sense reasoning in edge cases, and long-term planning without human guidance.
